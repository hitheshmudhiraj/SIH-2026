1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY AND OPERATIONAL CONTEXT
================================================================================

Indian Railways operates one of the largest and most congested rail networks in
the world. Running thousands of high-speed passenger trains, express services,
and freight trains requires continuous track, signalling, and electrical
maintenance. However, railway maintenance planning has traditionally operated in
rigid departmental silos:

1. Civil Engineering (Permanent Way / Track) manages track renewal, ballast
   tamping, rail welding, and track inspections through the Track Management
   System (TMS).
2. Signalling and Telecom (S&T) manages point machines, electronic
   interlocking, track circuits, and axle counters through the Signalling
   Maintenance Management System (SMMS).
3. Electrical Traction (TRD) manages 25kV Overhead Equipment (OHE), power
   cables, neutral sections, and substations through the Traction Distribution
   Management System (TDMS).
4. Operating Department controls train movements, line capacity, and schedule
   punctuality using the Control Office Application (COA).
5. Field Maintenance Crews submit local track possession requisitions through the
   Block Demand Management System (BDMS).

Because these five operational systems do not exchange structured data in real
time, maintenance blocks have historically been requested independently. This
causes four major operational failures:
- Repeated Track Closures: Engineering takes a 2-hour track block on Monday,
  S&T takes a 2-hour block on Tuesday, and Electrical takes a 2-hour block on
  Wednesday on the exact same 5-kilometer corridor, needlessly tripling track
  closure time.
- Heavy Machinery Bottlenecks: Highly expensive specialized machines, such as
  Plasser CSM 09-32 tamping machines or OHE tower wagons, are requested
  simultaneously by multiple sections, leading to idle crews or missed defects.
- Schedule Clashes with VIP Passenger Trains: Uncoordinated blocks overlap with
  high-priority trains such as the Vande Bharat Express or Rajdhani Express,
  leading to severe passenger delays and loss of punctuality metrics.
- Sub-Optimal Human Coordination: Controllers rely on phone calls and manual
  registers, making it difficult to find multi-department joint maintenance
  windows.

RailBlock AI solves these problems by providing an intelligent, transparent,
mathematically optimal decision-support platform. It integrates data from all
five silos, identifies joint maintenance opportunities, schedules maintenance
using constraint programming in under 100 milliseconds, predicts train delays
using machine learning, and maintains strict human-in-the-loop governance with
immutable cryptographic auditing.

CRITICAL OPERATIONAL PRINCIPLE:
RailBlock AI is strictly a decision-support platform. It does NOT autonomously
halt trains or grant track possessions. Every recommended plan must be reviewed,
validated, and authorized by certified railway personnel (Section Controllers,
Divisional Operations Managers, and Senior DOMs).

2.1 ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW AND CORE PRINCIPLES

The system follows a modern decoupled architecture designed for high
reliability, explainability, and speed:
- Modular Micro-Service Style Backend: Built with FastAPI in Python 3.11+,
  exposing clean REST APIs for ingestion, analysis, optimization, and auditing.
- Interactive High-Performance Frontend: Built with React 19, Vite, and
  Tailwind CSS, delivering an intuitive control-room user interface with Gantt
  charts, corridor topology maps, and explainability drawers.
- Dual Persistence Layer: Works with high-speed local CSV/JSON file repositories
  for zero-latency offline operations and syncs with cloud-hosted Supabase
  PostgreSQL with Row Level Security (RLS) for enterprise governance.
- Transparent Mathematical Optimization: Uses Google OR-Tools CP-SAT solver
  rather than opaque heuristics, guaranteeing clash-free schedules that satisfy
  every physical and operational constraint.
- Explainable Intelligence: Avoids black-box artificial intelligence; every
  priority score and schedule recommendation includes clear mathematical factor
  breakdowns that planners can inspect.


2.2 DETAILED MULTI-TIER ARCHITECTURE

The architecture is divided into five distinct operational tiers:

TIER 1: SILOED RAILWAY SOURCE SYSTEMS
- Track Management System (TMS): Provides civil engineering defect logs,
  ultrasonic rail flaw data (USFD), track geometry irregularities, and tamping
  requests.
- Signalling Maintenance Management System (SMMS): Supplies point machine health
  records, interlocking renewal requirements, and signal failure history.
- Traction Distribution Management System (TDMS): Provides 25kV OHE isolation
  schedules, tower wagon requirements, and contact wire height inspections.
- Control Office Application (COA): Delivers real-time and published passenger
  and freight train timetables, route speed limits, and train priority ranks.
- Block Demand Management System (BDMS): Ingests field requisition slips lodged
  by local permanent way inspectors (PWI) and signal supervisors.

TIER 2: DATA INTEGRATION HUB AND ASSET RECONCILIATION
- Located in backend services: data_integration.py and asset_registry.py.
- Runs a 13-step normalization pipeline to clean, format, and validate raw data.
- Canonical Asset Master Registry: Translates conflicting departmental names
  (e.g., "SW-POINT-102" in SMMS versus "PTS-102-UP" in TMS) into a single
  canonical asset ID linked to geographic coordinates and track chainage.
- Two-Tier Deduplication Engine: Eliminates duplicate block requests using exact
  canonical matching and spatial-temporal clustering (+/- 100 meters, +/- 2 days).
- Human Review Quarantine Queue: Isolates records with missing or anomalous data
  (such as missing duration or unknown station code) so planners can review and
  approve them before optimization.

TIER 3: ANALYTICS AND PRIORITIZATION ENGINES
- Located in backend services: priority_engine.py, corridor_availability.py, and
  opportunity_engine.py.
- 7-Factor Explainable Priority Model: Evaluates safety risk, asset criticality,
  overdue days, failure history, machine-learning failure probability, traffic
  density, and deferral consequence, outputting a clear 0-to-100 score.
- 30-Minute Corridor Headroom Profiler: Slices each 24-hour day into 48 discrete
  30-minute operational time windows across all 32 corridor sections. It
  calculates occupancy headroom based on scheduled train movements.
- VIP Timetable Blackout Engine: Strictly blocks maintenance scheduling during
  windows reserved for high-priority trains (Vande Bharat, Rajdhani).
- Opportunity Engine: Scans spatial-temporal neighbors to discover cross-
  departmental synergies (e.g., track renewal occurring within 2 km of signal
  point servicing under a planned OHE power shutdown).

TIER 4: MATHEMATICAL OPTIMIZATION AND MACHINE LEARNING ENGINES
- Located in backend: optimizer.py, block_planner.py, and ml/predict.py.
- Google OR-Tools CP-SAT Solver: Formulates block scheduling as a constraint
  satisfaction and optimization problem. Resolves all machine bottlenecks,
  enforces track possession exclusivity, and maximizes combined work output.
- Machine Learning Predictive Models (Scikit-Learn):
  * Gradient Boosting Regressor: Predicts anticipated train delays in minutes.
  * Random Forest Regressor: Predicts the total number of impacted train
    services (passenger and freight).
  * Gradient Boosting Regressor: Computes an overall schedule efficiency score.
- Dynamic Emergency Re-Planner: Handles real-time emergency events (such as
  sudden rail cracks) by running an incremental delta solver that inserts the
  emergency block while minimizing changes to the existing master schedule.

TIER 5: APPLICATION AND CONTROL-ROOM USER INTERFACE
- Backend REST API: Built using FastAPI and Pydantic, exposing documented
  endpoints (/api/integration, /api/optimizer, /api/replan, /api/review-queue).
- React Control-Room Interface:
  * Executive Dashboard: Live key performance indicators, active alerts, and
    pipeline status.
  * Siloed Intake View: Raw data feeds with data health indicators and review
    queue.
  * Unified Corridor Map: Interactive geographic layout of the 4 railway
    corridors and 32 sections.
  * 24-Hour Gantt Planning Board: Visual corridor timeline showing trains,
    available headroom slots, and planned maintenance blocks.
  * Explainability Waterfall Drawer: A dedicated pane showing why a block was
    scheduled, its 7-factor breakdown, and alternative rejected slots.
  * Before vs. After KPI Comparison: Measurable operational improvement graphs.
  * Immutable Audit Trail: Cryptographic chronological ledger of every human and
    algorithmic action.

2.4 DATA PERSISTENCE AND STORAGE ARCHITECTURE

RailBlock AI uses a hybrid, fault-tolerant persistence strategy:

1. Local File Repository (High Speed and Offline Capable):
   All incoming raw data, canonical registries, curated maintenance jobs,
   optimized plans, and audit trails are persisted as structured JSON and CSV
   files in the data directory. This ensures that the platform can function
   completely offline during network disruptions in divisional control offices.

2. Cloud Supabase PostgreSQL (Single Source of Truth):
   For enterprise multi-user deployments, the backend syncs with Supabase
   PostgreSQL. The schema defines tables for:
   - raw_block_requests: Incoming records from TMS, SMMS, TDMS, COA, and BDMS.
   - canonical_assets: Master asset register with latitude, longitude, and chainage.
   - maintenance_tasks: Curated work items with priority scores and requirements.
   - scheduled_blocks: Approved blocks with start/end times, corridors, and crews.
   - audit_trail: Append-only ledger recording planner IDs, timestamps, and
     cryptographic hashes.
   - Row Level Security (RLS) is enabled on all tables to enforce role-based
     access (Section Controller, Sr. DOM, Auditor).

3. COMPLETE STEP-BY-STEP WORKING FLOW
================================================================================

The end-to-end operation of RailBlock AI progresses through thirteen distinct
phases:

STAGE 1: MULTI-SYSTEM INGESTION AND RAW FEED INTAKE
- The backend file repository scans and ingests raw defect registers and block
  requisitions from five external legacy systems:
  * TMS: Permanent way ultrasonic rail defects, rail joint renewals, ballast
    tamping requisitions.
  * SMMS: Point machine motor overhauls, track circuit testing, signal renewal.
  * TDMS: 25kV OHE contact wire tensioning, bracket insulator replacements.
  * COA: Train schedules (Vande Bharat, Rajdhani, Express, local freight).
  * BDMS: Daily field block demands filed by sectional engineers.
- Feeds arrive via batch CSV files or REST webhooks into data/01_raw_sources/.

STAGE 2: 13-STEP NORMALIZATION AND DATA CLEANING PIPELINE
- Raw inputs vary in date formats, naming conventions, and completeness.
  The data integration service runs a strict 13-step standardization pipeline:
  Step 1 : Stripping leading and trailing whitespace from string fields.
  Step 2 : Standardizing corridor identifiers (mapping aliases like "Corridor-1"
           or "c1" to canonical ID "C01").
  Step 3 : Standardizing section IDs to uppercase canonical codes (e.g.,
           "SEC_C01_02").
  Step 4 : Converting all date strings into ISO 8601 standard (YYYY-MM-DD).
  Step 5 : Normalizing time fields to 24-hour HH:MM format.
  Step 6 : Validating duration fields (must be a positive integer in minutes;
           if missing or zero, flagged with smart default of 60 minutes).
  Step 7 : Department classification validation (Engineering, Signalling,
           Traction).
  Step 8 : Work type categorization (Track Maintenance, Point Machine Overhaul,
           OHE Power Block, Signal Testing).
  Step 9 : Chainage and kilometer verification (converting kilometer points into
           floating point numbers and verifying they fall within section boundaries).
  Step 10: Track direction standardization (UP, DOWN, BOTH, SLOW, FAST).
  Step 11: Machine and resource requirement mapping (CSM tamping machine, tower
           wagon, welding gang).
  Step 12: Schema compliance verification against required data fields.
  Step 13: Generating a unique universally unique identifier (UUID) for each
           valid job record.

STAGE 3: CANONICAL ASSET MASTER REGISTRY AND SPATIAL RECONCILIATION
- Different departments frequently refer to the exact same physical asset using
  inconsistent names. For example, a track turn-out might be recorded as
  "Turnout-24B" by Civil Engineering, but "Point-Machine-24B" by Signalling.
- The asset registry service groups assets located within a spatial radius of
  +/- 100 meters on the same track corridor.
- It assigns a single Canonical Asset Identifier that links all cross-departmental
  records to a shared geographic location, station, and chainage marker.

STAGE 4: TWO-TIER DEDUPLICATION AND REVIEW QUARANTINE QUEUE
- Maintenance requests often contain duplicates due to re-submissions.
- The system runs a two-tier deduplication algorithm:
  * Tier 1 (Exact Canonical Match): If two records share the same canonical
    asset ID, work category, and requested date, the newer record is retained,
    prioritizing official system updates in the order TMS > SMMS > TDMS > BDMS.
  * Tier 2 (Near-Match Clustering): Identifies jobs within 100 meters and within
    48 hours of each other. Potential duplicates are flagged for verification.
- Incomplete Records Quarantine: Records with missing critical data (such as an
  unrecognized station code) are not deleted. Instead, they are quarantined
  into the Human Review Queue (invalid_records.json). Planners can open the
  Review Queue Panel in the UI, edit the missing fields, and promote the record
  to the active planning pool.

STAGE 5: EXPLAINABLE 7-FACTOR PRIORITY SCORING ENGINE
- Each validated maintenance job is evaluated by the prioritization engine.
- Instead of using an opaque black-box neural network, it applies a transparent
  mathematical scoring model yielding an integer priority score from 0 to 100:
  1. Safety Risk Score (0 to 25 points): Evaluates derailment risk, fractured
     rails, or red-flag safety defects.
  2. Asset Criticality Score (0 to 20 points): Evaluates importance of track
     class (Mainline vs. Loop line vs. Yard siding).
  3. Overdue Maintenance Days (0 to 15 points): Scales with days passed beyond
     statutory maintenance due date.
  4. Failure History Score (0 to 15 points): High repeat breakdown assets receive
     higher priority.
  5. Predicted Failure Probability (0 to 15 points): Calibrated output from a
     scikit-learn Logistic Regression model trained on historical component wear.
  6. Traffic Density Score (0 to 10 points): Corridors handling more than 80
     trains per day receive maximum density weighting.
  7. Deferral Consequence Score (0 to 10 points): Evaluates speed restriction
     penalties (e.g., imposing a 30 km/h caution order).
- Every score generates an explainable breakdown stored alongside the job.
  When a planner clicks "Why?" on any task in the UI, an interactive waterfall
  drawer displays the exact points contributed by each factor.

STAGE 6: CORRIDOR HEADROOM AND TIMETABLE BLACKOUT CALCULATION
- The corridor availability engine ingests 160 passenger and freight train paths
  from COA.
- It discretizes each corridor into 48 half-hour slots per 24-hour day across
  all 32 sections.
- For each 30-minute slot, it calculates train headway and occupancy:
  * 0 trains scheduled : Availability Score = 1.0 (Maximum headroom).
  * 1 train scheduled  : Availability Score = 0.8 (Good headroom).
  * 2 trains scheduled : Availability Score = 0.5 (Moderate congestion).
  * 3+ trains scheduled: Availability Score = 0.25 (Heavy congestion).
- VIP Train Protected Blackouts: High-priority trains (Vande Bharat Express,
  Rajdhani Express) have strict non-negotiable blackout buffers. No maintenance
  block may be scheduled during their passage window plus a 20-minute safety
  headway buffer.

STAGE 7: CROSS-DEPARTMENT SYNERGY AND JOINT OPPORTUNITY DISCOVERY
- The opportunity engine scans all prioritized tasks across departments.
- When it detects that an Electrical Traction task requires an OHE 25kV power
  shutdown on Section SEC_C01_02, it immediately searches for:
  * Track renewals on the same section.
  * Signal point overhauls within 2 kilometers.
- It bundles these compatible jobs into a "Joint Shadow Megablock Candidate"
  (e.g., Opportunity OPP-007). Performing these jobs together means the track
  is blocked only once instead of three separate times.

STAGE 8: MATHEMATICAL BLOCK SCHEDULING (GOOGLE OR-TOOLS CP-SAT)
- The block planner translates the maintenance requirements, corridor slots,
  machine rosters, and joint opportunities into a Constraint Programming (CP-SAT)
  mathematical model:
  * Decision Variables: Boolean indicator variables determining whether job i
    is scheduled in time slot t on corridor c with resource r.
  * Hard Constraint 1 (Track Exclusivity): No two conflicting work items may
    occupy the same track segment unless they are formally certified as joint
    shadow block partners.
  * Hard Constraint 2 (Train Blackout Protection): No block may overlap with a
    VIP train operational window.
  * Hard Constraint 3 (Resource Concurrency): A specialized machine (such as a
    Plasser CSM 09-32 tamping machine or tower wagon) can only be at one place
    at any given time. Crew transit speed between sections is enforced.
  * Hard Constraint 4 (Duration Integrity): A 180-minute task must receive 6
    consecutive 30-minute slots without interruption.
  * Multi-Criteria Objective Function:
    Maximize: Sum of Task Priority Scores
            + 150 points bonus for each successfully formed Joint Megablock
            + 50 points bonus for utilizing high-headroom off-peak slots
            - 100 points penalty for each minute of track possession taken
            - 500 points penalty for deferring any high-risk critical defect.
- The solver executes and reaches provable optimality or high-quality solution
  in approximately 70 milliseconds, outputting a coordinated 7-day master plan.

STAGE 9: MACHINE LEARNING DELAY AND OPERATIONAL IMPACT PREDICTION
- Once the CP-SAT solver creates the draft schedule, the schedule is passed
  to the Machine Learning inference pipeline (ml/predict.py):
  * Gradient Boosting Regressor (120 trees): Predicts total cumulative passenger
    and freight delay in minutes for every planned block.
  * Random Forest Regressor (100 trees): Predicts the exact number of train
    services affected or diverted.
  * Secondary Gradient Boosting Regressor: Computes a standardized Block
    Efficiency Score (0 to 100) reflecting capacity utilization.
- These predictive metrics give railway planners immediate foresight into how
  each maintenance possession will impact divisional punctuality before the
  possession order is signed.

STAGE 10: HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP GOVERNANCE, PLANNER REVIEW, AND SIGN-OFF
- The schedule is published to the React Control-Room UI in a pending review
  state.
- Railway Section Controllers and Planners inspect the plan through the visual
  Gantt board and corridor map:
  * Planners can inspect individual blocks, read the explainability rationale,
    and review the ML-predicted train delays.
  * If operational conditions require changes (e.g., bad weather or VIP
    special train movement), the planner can manually drag and adjust block
    timings.
  * Mandatory Operational Justification: Whenever a planner alters an
    optimized block or overrides a system recommendation, the system forces
    them to select an official justification code (e.g., "WEATHER_ADVISORY",
    "VIP_MOVEMENT", "CREW_UNAVAILABLE") and enter detailed remarks.
  * Senior Divisional Operating Manager (Sr. DOM) Sign-off: The plan enters
    official status only when authorized with digital credentials.

STAGE 11: FIELD DISPATCH AND WORK POSSESSION ORDERS
- Once authorized, the finalized master plan is exported as:
  * Digital Work Possession Orders (formatted CSV/JSON) transmitted to Section
    Controllers and Station Masters.
  * Machine Movement Notices sent to tamping machine supervisors and tower wagon
    depots.
  * Notice to COA for train regulation notices and cautionary speed warnings.

STAGE 12: DYNAMIC EMERGENCY RE-PLANNING (ULTRASONIC RAIL FRACTURES)
- Real-world operations face unexpected track emergencies, such as an ultrasonic
  rail fracture (USFD) detected on the mainline at KM 124.4.
- In the UI, the planner can simulate or inject an emergency defect.
- The emergency defect is immediately assigned a critical priority (98/100).
- The Incremental Delta Re-Planner (replanner.py) is triggered:
  * It freezes all currently executing or near-term blocks.
  * It clears a 120-minute emergency slot by shifting non-critical lower-
    priority maintenance tasks to subsequent days.
  * It generates "Plan V2" with automated diff tracking:
    - ADDED: Emergency rail crack repair block.
    - MOVED: Deferrable tamping task rescheduled to next daylight window.
    - UNCHANGED: 85% of existing stable maintenance blocks remain untouched.
  * This prevents whole-system chaos by applying a minimal-churn solver policy.

STAGE 13: CRYPTOGRAPHIC AUDIT LOGGING AND TRACEABILITY
- Every event across the entire lifecycle is logged in the append-only audit
  store (data/05_audit_logs/audit_events.json):
  * Event timestamp, user ID, user role, and workstation IP.
  * Action type (DATA_INGESTED, QUARANTINE_EDIT, SOLVER_RUN, PLAN_MODIFIED,
    PLAN_APPROVED, EMERGENCY_INJECTED).
  * Cryptographic SHA-256 hash chaining ensuring that historical audit entries
    cannot be altered or backdated.
  * Provides complete legal and operational accountability for railway safety
    inquiries and Commissioner of Railway Safety (CRS) inspections.

5.1 THE 7-FACTOR EXPLAINABLE PRIORITIZATION FORMULA

To eliminate arbitrary scheduling and opaque black-box bias, every maintenance
job is assigned a mathematical score P between 0 and 100 points:

P = S_risk + A_crit + O_days + F_hist + P_fail + T_dens + D_cons

Where:
1. S_risk (Safety Risk, 0 to 25 points):
   - Critical fracture or structural emergency: 25 points
   - High derailment risk / red flag: 18 to 22 points
   - Medium wear or routine defect: 10 to 15 points
   - Preventive cosmetic maintenance: 2 to 5 points
2. A_crit (Asset Criticality, 0 to 20 points):
   - Mainline high-speed track or junction point: 20 points
   - Intermediate block section: 14 to 17 points
   - Loop lines or secondary sidings: 6 to 10 points
3. O_days (Overdue Days, 0 to 15 points):
   - Calculated as: minimum(15, (Current_Date - Statutory_Due_Date) * 0.5)
   - Tasks overdue by 30 days or more receive the maximum 15 points.
4. F_hist (Failure History, 0 to 15 points):
   - Evaluates repeat failures on the same asset within the last 180 days:
     Score = minimum(15, Repeat_Breakdowns * 3.75)
5. P_fail (Predicted Failure Probability, 0 to 15 points):
   - Output from a trained Logistic Regression model based on age, tonnage,
     and weather stress:
     Score = round(Failure_Probability * 15)
6. T_dens (Traffic Density, 0 to 10 points):
   - High density corridors (> 80 trains/day): 10 points
   - Medium density (40 to 80 trains/day): 6 to 8 points
   - Low density (< 40 trains/day): 2 to 4 points
7. D_cons (Deferral Consequence, 0 to 10 points):
   - Speed restriction imposition (caution order <= 30 km/h): 10 points
   - Minor speed restriction (caution order 50-75 km/h): 5 to 7 points
   - No immediate operational penalty: 1 to 3 points

The total score P is clamped between 0 and 100. Every factor value is stored
as metadata to provide complete transparency.


5.2 CORRIDOR AVAILABILITY HEADROOM MODEL

Each 24-hour operational day is divided into 48 discrete slots of 30 minutes
each. For any section s and slot t:
Availability_Score(s, t) = 1.0 - Headway_Occupancy_Penalty(s, t)

Penalty Rules:
- If scheduled trains in slot = 0: Penalty = 0.0  (Headroom = 1.0)
- If scheduled trains in slot = 1: Penalty = 0.2  (Headroom = 0.8)
- If scheduled trains in slot = 2: Penalty = 0.5  (Headroom = 0.5)
- If scheduled trains in slot >= 3: Penalty = 0.75 (Headroom = 0.25)
- If any VIP train (priority >= 9, e.g. Vande Bharat) passes through slot t or
  adjacent buffer slot (t - 1, t + 1):
  Availability_Score(s, t) = 0.0 (NON-NEGOTIABLE BLACKOUT - HARD PROHIBITION)


5.3 GOOGLE OR-TOOLS CP-SAT MATHEMATICAL MODEL

Mathematical Formulation:
Let T be the set of maintenance tasks, B be the set of 30-minute block slots,
C be the set of corridor sections, and R be the set of specialized machines.

Decision Variables:
- x[i, t] in {0, 1}: 1 if task i starts at time slot t; 0 otherwise.
- y[i]: 1 if task i is scheduled in the plan; 0 if deferred.
- z[c, t] in {0, 1}: 1 if corridor section c is blocked at time slot t.
- joint[i, j] in {0, 1}: 1 if compatible tasks i and j are bundled into a joint block.

Constraints:
1. Exact Duration:
   If task i starts at t, it occupies slots from t to (t + duration[i] - 1).
   For all occupied slots tau, z[section[i], tau] must equal 1.
2. Exclusive Track Possession:
   For any corridor section c and slot t, at most one independent department
   possession may occur unless marked as a certified joint block.
3. Machine Non-Concurrency:
   For any machine r and slot t:
   Sum of all tasks using machine r at slot t <= 1.
4. VIP Passenger Train Protection:
   If slot t has VIP train blackout on section c, then z[c, t] = 0.

Objective Function:
Maximize:
  Sum(P[i] * y[i])
  + 150 * Sum(joint[i, j])
  + 50 * Sum(Availability_Score(section[i], t) * x[i, t])
  - 100 * (Total_Track_Hours_Blocked)
  - 500 * Sum(P[i] * (1 - y[i]) for all critical tasks i)


5.4 MACHINE LEARNING PREDICTION PIPELINE

Model 1: Train Delay Regressor
- Algorithm: GradientBoostingRegressor with 120 estimators, max depth of 4,
  learning rate of 0.08.
- Input Features: Section ID, corridor traffic density, time of day (daylight
  vs. night), duration in minutes, number of passenger trains in adjacent slots.
- Target Output: Cumulative passenger and freight train delay in minutes (R-squared = 0.95).

Model 2: Affected Services Regressor
- Algorithm: RandomForestRegressor with 100 trees, min samples split of 3.
- Target Output: Total count of passenger and freight train paths that require
  regulation, speed restriction, or platform reallocation.

Model 3: Block Efficiency Classifier / Regressor
- Algorithm: GradientBoostingRegressor with normalized loss.
- Target Output: Composite efficiency score (0 to 100) combining capacity
  headroom, joint work density, and punctuality protection.


5.5 INCREMENTAL EMERGENCY RE-PLANNING DELTA LOGIC

When a critical defect (e.g., Ultrasonic Rail Flaw USFD, Priority 98/100) is
detected:
1. Lock Near-Term Horizon: Blocks scheduled to start within the next 60 minutes
   or currently underway are frozen and cannot be moved.
2. Defect Insertion: The emergency task is immediately inserted into the
   earliest feasible daylight slot on the defect's section.
3. Conflict Shift: If an existing routine task occupies that slot, the solver
   searches for the closest subsequent slot with available headroom and crew
   availability.
4. Minimal Churn Objective: The solver minimizes the number of moved tasks:
   Minimize: Cost_Emergency_Delay + 10 * Number_Of_Moved_Existing_Blocks
5. Automated Diff Categorization:
   Every block in Plan V2 is labeled:
   - ADDED: The emergency maintenance item.
   - MOVED: Lower-priority task pushed to a later window.
   - UNCHANGED: Stable tasks that remain at their original scheduled times.
